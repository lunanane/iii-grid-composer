Manual

A monome iii script for live performance and composition — built around a chord & arp sequencer based on harmonic-mixing principles, alongside three mono sequencers, a polyrhythmic drum sequencer, and a performance page with animated faders plus mute and transpose control over the individual sequencers. Every bit of that state saves and recalls from 127 scene slots, quantised to the bar.

What it is

Grid Composer is an iii script for the monome grid, conceptually descended from the 12-year-old Grid Ninja. It revisits that live set with one long-wanted addition: a chord/arp tracker-style sequencer that defines a lead scale for the rest of the instrument, making genuine scale progressions playable rather than fixing everything to a single pre-selected key.

The chord/arp engine borrows the step-capture idea from the Polyend Tracker. Each tracker step stores not just a chord but its key, scale, groove and velocity pattern, so an eight-stage progression can move through harmony as well as rhythm.

Three further sequencers sit around it. A polyrhythmic drum sequencer provides per-track loop lengths, ratcheting and snap-back to MIDI clock. A performance page, informed by the OXI One, gathers global transpose, every mute, and eight animated faders whose eased timing drives fast, musical parameter changes. And three monophonic sequencers, each locked to the lead scale, provide simple melodic lines with their own velocity patterns.

DISCLAIMER. The script was developed with the help of Gemini and Claude, primarily to fit this much functionality into the very limited memory of an iii script. My role was essentially that of UI designer; each round of feature work was followed by an AI-assisted compression pass.
New in c30. The menu key is momentary as well as latching. Fader banks 1–4 can be raised over any page from the menu row. The arp has a gate-length control with legato. Grooves and swing are locked to the bar, and the mono sequencers have twelve bar-aligned step rates. Three things behave differently from earlier builds: writing a tracker stage now needs a held chord; pad (3,3) of the Playing Area is C♯ major (3B), where it used to repeat D♯; and a scene saved on an older build may play a mono line at a different rate — see §05.

00 Basics

Conventions

Coordinates are (column, row) — columns 1–16 left→right, rows 1–8 top→bottom.

Clock. Grid Composer follows external MIDI clock (24 PPQN) with start / continue / stop. Start resets every page to the top; stop silences everything. The Tracker-Sequencer also has a local Run/Stop at (13,8).

Menu. (16,8) is the menu key. Tap it to latch menu mode on, and tap again to leave. Hold it for longer than about a fifth of a second and the menu stays open only while you hold, closing on release — so hold + a page pad changes page with one hand. In menu mode row 8 becomes the page selector (1–4, 8) and the fader-bank quick access (8–11, 8), and each page reveals extra controls (shown in its menu schematic).

Scenes. (1,8) on the Perform page opens the scene launcher: 127 slots that store and recall the whole instrument. See §05.

Animated faders & banks — (9–16, *)

Eight vertical faders. Press any cell to set a target; the value eases to it over ~0.5 s, independent of clock — that's the rhythmical fast-parameter feel. Each fader streams a MIDI CC on channel 1. The menu switches between 5 fader banks, so the eight faders address CC 20–27, 28–35, 36–43, 44–51 and 52–59 in turn.

Mutes & transpose — (1–8, 1–7)

Row 1 mutes the six drum tracks. Rows 2–5 are the Arp and Mono 1–3: column 1 mutes the voice, columns 2–8 transpose it (0 at centre column 5, ±3 steps). Row 6 is the multiplier ×1–×8 — how many semitones each transpose step is worth. Row 7 is a global transpose over everything.

Scenes — (1,8)

The one lit pad on row 8 opens the scene launcher, which takes over the left 8×8 while the faders keep running on the right. Full description in §05.

Fader banks from any page — (8–11, 8) in menu

In menu mode on any page, (8–11, 8) raise fader bank 1–4 over the right half of the grid, on top of whatever page is showing. The page underneath stays selected and keeps playing. 03 Page three

Chord/Arp Tracker-Sequencer

This is an accumulation of me trying to understand chord progression.

There are concepts in here inspired by Arplus by Zlosynth (check out their amazing module and manual), and the Chordality app, which explores the same ideas by arranging chords on the circle of filth (the Camelot Wheel). I've worked with harmonic mixing for ages in my DJ sets and live-set planning, and wanted to finally integrate it into a live playing instrument. A limitation many sequencers have — like the amazing Polyend synth, also an inspiration — is that you can't progress between scales; you only play within one scale once it's set. Here, you can.

Start here — the three play modes

It's maybe not obvious at first, but there are multiple levels of playability, switched on the Playmode Modifier at column 14, rows 5–7:

1
Scale arrangement — the default. Each pad in the Playing Area is its own key (the Camelot layout); pressing one sets the working scale and plays the selected chord in it. This is how you move through scale progressions.
2
Locked scale — locks the last-played scale. You now play diatonically within it, in the same circle-of-filth arrangement, so every pad gives a chord that belongs to that one key. Lock it by holding the centre pad (14,6) and pressing a key.
3
Note grid — inspired by how the Plinky handles custom chords. Still locked to the scale you last played, but the layout changes: notes rise within each column and each column is offset from the next, so you can hold any combination and play fully in-key custom voicings. Plinky-light. Here chord settings don't apply, but arp settings do.

Levels 1 and 2 both draw from the Chord/Arp Settings: they either play the selected chord directly, or — when the arpeggiator is active — use that chord as the source for the arp.

The page at a glance

Seven regions: the Playing Area (chord roots / scale keys) with the Chord Type pads beneath it, the Chord/Arp Settings (type, speed, direction, voicing size), the Groove and Velocity Patterns, the Tracker-Sequencer, and the Playmode Modifiers with swing, inversion, octave, Run/Stop and gate.

The Camelot Wheel

The Playing Area is the Camelot Wheel of harmonic mixing, cut open at the top and bottom and straightened into two halves. Columns 1–2 are the left half of the wheel, running from 12 at the top down to 7; columns 3–4 are the right half, running from 1 down to 6. Within each pair the first column is the major key (outer ring, B) and the second is its relative minor (inner ring, A), so two pads side by side share a Camelot number and a key signature.

That gives the grid the same moves a DJ makes on the wheel. One pad up or down is one step round the wheel, a fifth away. One pad sideways within a pair swaps major for relative minor. The halves join where the wheel was cut: the top of column 1 (12B) is next door to the top of column 3 (1B), and the bottom of column 1 (7B) to the bottom of column 3 (6B). Hover over a pad or a wedge, or tap it, to light up its partners. The pads are spelled with sharps as on the other diagrams, the wheel with its usual flats — C♯ is D♭, G♯ is A♭, and so on.

Tracker-Sequencer — (9–12, 1–8)

Eight stages, top to bottom. Hold a chord in the Playing Area and press a cell to write that chord and all its settings — chord type, key, scale, groove, velocity, speed, direction — into that stage; the column (1–4) sets the stage's duration in bars. Running, the stages chain into a progression. A press with no chord held writes nothing, so a stray tap in the middle of a set cannot overwrite a stage. Hold a stage to edit it; in menu mode, tap a stage to clear it. This per-step capture of groove, velocity, chord type, key and scale is the part I'm proudest of.

A stage also remembers which play mode wrote it. Capture while you are in the note grid (mode 3) and the stage stores the exact voicing you were holding — up to six notes — and replays those notes instead of rebuilding a chord from root + type. So one progression can mix Camelot chords and hand-built note-grid voicings, stage by stage.

Chord type — (1–4, 7–8)

Eight single-button types; hold the ROOT pad (1,7) and tap another for the extended colours. While a root is held, changing the type re-voices live. Full tables in §06.

Chord/Arp Settings & modifiers

At (5–8, 1–4): type (chord / arp / arp+octave / off), speed (1/8, 1/8 triplet, 1/16, 1/16 triplet), direction (up / down / up-down / pattern), voicing size (5/4/3/2). At (13–16, 5–7): Swing (col 13), Playmode (col 14), Inversion dial (col 15), Key octave (col 16). Run/Stop is (13,8) and gate length is (14,8).

Swing (13, 5–7) is off / +1 / +2. It holds every second step of the groove and of the arp back by one or two MIDI clock pulses (of 24 per quarter), so the pattern lands late and lazy without changing tempo. The delayed steps are placed from the bar position, so swing never drifts against the drums however long a chord is held. Swing is stored per scene, not per stage.

Gate (14,8) sets how long each arp note and each struck chord rings. Each press steps through normal (about 120 ms, pad mid-bright), short (about 60 ms, pad dim) and legato (pad full-bright), where a note is held until the next one starts. Gate length is stored per scene.

Mono Sequencers

Three independent monophonic lines on channels 4–6, each with its own velocity patterns — all locked to the lead scale from the Tracker-Sequencer. Each line can be up to eight pages of 16 steps long, and can tie repeated notes instead of retriggering them.

Menu: (16,1–3) selects the sequencer; row 1 sets the step speed on columns 1–12, slow to fast — 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48 and 96 steps per bar, with column 8 (16 per bar) the default. Every rate divides the bar evenly, so a line holds its place against the other pages; columns 13–15 are unused. Then: row 7 the velocity pattern; (15,5) / (15,6) scroll the pitch window up and down; (14,6) clears the line; rows 2–5 still edit notes. Row 6 is now the page strip, and (9,6) is tie mode.

Sequence pages — (1–8, 6) in menu

Each mono line can run up to eight pages of 16 steps — 128 steps at full length. The eight pads on row 6 of the menu are that page strip, and they use the same two gestures as the drum page:

▸
Tap a pad to choose the edit page — the one the pitch grid is showing. It blinks.
▸
Hold pg1 and press pgN to set the page count to N. Pages beyond the count are nearly dark; shortening the line pulls the edit and playing pages back inside it.

Pages chain by traversal, not by bar: one pass of the loop window plays one page, then the line steps to the next page and wraps after the last one. So the row-8 loop region sets how long each page lasts, and the page count sets how many of them the line walks through before repeating. The currently playing page shows mid-bright on the strip, the edit page blinks — which is how you can edit page 4 while page 2 is sounding.

Because the pitch grid always shows the edit page, its playhead only appears when the edit page happens to be the playing page. Row 8 keeps its playhead running either way, so you can always see time moving. A MIDI start resets all three lines to page 1, loop start. (14,6) clears everything: all pages, back to one page and a full 1–16 loop.

Tie mode — (9,6) in menu

Per track, and it blinks while armed. Off (the default) every step re-attacks, as it always did. On, a step that resolves to the same MIDI note already sounding is left ringing instead of being retriggered — so consecutive identical steps become one long note, and that works across a page boundary too. A rest always breaks the tie, and so does anything that changes the resolved pitch: transposing the track, a global transpose, or the lead scale moving under it will all retrigger. Returning to page 1 — the restart of the whole sequence — also always retriggers.

Slots and banks

Rows 1–4 are 32 slot pads, numbered left→right and top→bottom. Row 6 switches between four banks of those, for 128 positions of which 127 are real — the very last pad of bank 4 stays dark. Tap a slot to select it; tap it again to deselect. Nothing happens until you then press one of the three action pads on row 8.

PadAction
(3,8) LOADRecall the selected scene. Stays dim unless the selected slot holds one.
(4,8) SAVEWrite the current state into the selected slot, new or overwriting.
(5,8) DELETEErase the selected scene and free the slot. Dim unless the slot holds one.
(1,8) ←Close the launcher and go back to the Perform controls.

Recall is quantised

This is the part that matters live. With the clock running, LOAD does not fire immediately — it arms the scene, the pad blinks from black, and the swap lands on the next bar. With the clock stopped it loads straight away. Either way the instrument is silenced first — chord, arp, all three mono lines and every drum note get their note-offs — so a scene change can never leave a note hanging.

Two details that keep the transition musical. The faders don't jump: a recall sets their targets and they ease across over ~0.5 s from wherever they were, exactly as if you had pressed them. And if the Tracker-Sequencer was running, it re-fires its stage into the new harmony rather than waiting for the next stage boundary.

Playheads, menu state, which slot is selected and any half-finished hold gesture are deliberately not stored — they are rebuilt on recall, which is why a scene can land mid-song without stalling the clock.

Older scenes still load. Scenes are versioned and only ever gain fields on the end. A preset saved before the mono pages existed still recalls correctly — those lines simply come back as a single page with tie and swing off and the gate at normal. One thing does change: the mono step-speed row was rebuilt in c30. Speed pads 6 and 8–11 mean what they always did, but an older scene that used any other speed pad now plays that line at the rate the pad has today — pick the speed again and re-save.
Why saving feels like a hiccup. Writing to the device's flash is far slower than a grid frame, so the script does at most one file operation per ~30 ms tick and then waits. A save or a load costs a few frames of LED refresh and nothing else — the clock handler never touches storage, so no pulse is ever dropped. The slot map itself is read once at startup from a single small index file rather than by opening all 127, so the occupancy lights appear a moment after the script comes up.

Inversion & octave

The inversion dial (15,5–7) rotates voicings; the key-octave radio (16,5–7) shifts the whole chord ±1 octave. Both apply to whatever is playing.

07 Reference

Grooves & velocity

Rhythm grooves — 16 gate patterns

Each groove is a 16-step on/off gate (lit = note fires) applied to the Chord/Arp page. Pattern 1 is every step. 2 and 7 split the eighths into odd and even; 4, 8 and 16 are quarter-note pulses at three different offsets; 3, 9 and 10 space their hits three steps apart, which pulls a triplet feel across the 16-step frame; 12 and 13 are deliberately sparse, two hits in sixteen; and 5, 6, 11, 14, 15 are denser syncopated figures. The selected groove pad pulses in time as it plays, and the swing control at (13, 5–7) drags every second step of whichever one is running.

A groove is read against the bar, not against a running count: it restarts on every downbeat, so it sits in the same place every bar. How much of the pattern you hear follows the speed. At 1/16 all sixteen steps play once per bar; at 1/8 the first eight; at the triplet speeds the first twelve — once per bar at 1/8 triplet, twice at 1/16 triplet.

Velocity patterns — the function

Velocity is computed, not stored — sixteen accent shapes from one global function vgv(g, i, base), where g is the pattern (1–16), i the step, base the nominal velocity. Pattern 1 returns the base flat; the rest map a shape sh ∈ [0,1] into velocity 76–108 (≈ 60–85%).

-- g=1 → flat. n = g-1, p = step phase (0..15)
if n <= 4  -- hard accents: every k-th step (k = 4,2,8,3)
   k = {4,2,8,3}[n];      sh = (p % k == 0) ? 1 : 0.15
elseif n <= 8  -- offset / syncopated accents (phase-shifted)
   sh = ((p - offset) % k == 0) ? 1 : 0.2
elseif n <= 12 -- smooth swells
   sh = (math.sin(p/16 · f · 2π) + 1) · 0.5
else           -- build-ups: quarter-note pulse ∨ sine
   sh = max( p%4==0 ? 1 : 0 , (math.sin(…) + 1)·0.35 )
return math.floor(76 + 32 · sh + 0.5)
By band: patterns 2–5 are hard parity/quarter accents, 6–9 offset accents, 10–13 sine swells, 14–16 build-ups. No lookup tables — it's all math.sin and phase modulo, which is what keeps it inside the iii memory budget.
Note length. Drum hits are short triggers. Chord and arp note length is set by the gate pad (14,8) on the Chord/Arp page — normal, short or legato.
